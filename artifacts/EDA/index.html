EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS¶

What this section does:

Now we interrogate the data to understand it deeply. The goal is not just to produce charts. The goal is to build a fact-based foundation for every claim we make in the audit findings.

We examine five things:

  1. Transaction type breakdown — where does fraud actually live?
  2. Time patterns — does fraud cluster at specific hours or days?
  3. Amount distribution — do fraudulent transactions look different in size?
  4. Zero balance logic check — how often does fraud result in a completely drained account?
  5. Feature correlations — are any of our planned model features redundant or problematic?

Each finding directly informs a decision made later in the audit.

EDA findings and The Invisible 41¶

EDA Summary Findings¶

Five facts have been established from the raw data before any model is trained:

Fact 1 — Fraud is structurally confined. Only TRANSFER and CASH_OUT transactions contain fraud. PAYMENT, CASH_IN, and DEBIT have zero fraud cases across 6.3 million transactions. This is not noise — it is a structural property of how fraud operates. Fraudsters use exit routes.

Fact 2 — Fraud has temporal signal. The step column, converted to hour of day, shows fraud clusters heavily between 3am and 6am. This is the window when account holders are asleep and least able to intervene. The step column carries predictive value and will be retained as a model feature.

Fact 3 — Fraud is large-amount, account-draining behaviour. Fraudulent transactions are significantly larger than legitimate ones on average. The dominant pattern is account liquidation: a large amount moved, then the origin balance hits exactly zero.

Fact 4 — The zero-balance signature is powerful. Fraudulent transactions result in a zero origin balance at a rate far higher than legitimate ones. This will become one of the most important features in the model.

Fact 5 — Feature correlations are manageable. No destructive collinearity was found. We will engineer difference-based features to capture balance movement more directly.

The Discovery — The Invisible 41¶

When we break the fraud data down by account balance tier, the picture becomes clear.

Fraud in this dataset is not evenly distributed. It is concentrated almost entirely in high-balance accounts.

Balance Group Fraud Cases Share of All Fraud
High-Balance 7,150 87.06%
Mid-Balance 1,022 12.44%
Low-Balance 41 0.50%

Out of 8,213 total fraud cases, only 41 come from low-balance users. That is half a percent.

This creates a direct problem for any model trained on this data. When the model trains, it will study 7,150 examples of what fraud looks like in high-balance accounts. For low-balance users, it has 41 examples. Those 41 cases are so outnumbered they barely register as a pattern worth learning.

Imagine training a doctor to diagnose an illness using 7,150 patient files from wealthy neighbourhoods and 41 files from low-income neighbourhoods. That doctor will become skilled at diagnosing illness in the first group. When a patient from the second group walks in, the doctor is not incompetent. They are simply undertrained for that specific context. They may miss the diagnosis entirely, not because they stopped caring, but because they never had enough examples to learn from.

That is the situation this model is walking into. We call this the Invisible 41. The fraud happened. The people are real. But from the model's perspective, their cases are statistical noise.
